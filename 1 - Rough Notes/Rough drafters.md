A "Rough Drafter" is a joke I started at a friend's house party 4-5 months into living in Austin.
It refers to the type of people who (uncannily common in Austin) would crash into conversations way over their heads. All rant; no rhyme or reason, and confident as a bull. They will proudly present their first (very) rough draft to anyone who will listen. 

It's Dunning-Kruger at the absolute most insufferable.

Here's the story:

My friends here all know each other from the Sapien Center. This was a health-club/social-club backyard gym/co-working space in east Austin. People go there to hang out and talk like-minded on ancestral diets, holistic wellness, and basically whatever else fringe health studies are gaining awareness at the time.

They're all standing around a table, talking about meat or sunlight or whatever when somebody's hippie cousin came in. With his vast stores of awareness, he could tell we were ready for spiritual wisdom. He introduced himself with "I think testosterone is overrated."

Now, thinking this was an invite to fun discussion or just a bit of mischief, he gets humored and the conversation steers in that direction. but no, this guy was serious. He wasn't your average spiritual bro, he was level 65 shaman.

The guy dragged on, heavy-handing any and all talk he possibly could into the realm of psychedelic dreams and tHe natUre of CoNSciousNess. Yet not a word of what this guy said made any sense! Feeling sly and more than a little annoyed at our eager-to-be conversation leader, I tried hinting to him that perhaps he should pay more attention to his audience. None of us could follow him, so I disguised this gripe into a little metaphor and challenged him to package a single thing he was trying to say into a simple, finished gemstone.

He took my hint and *ran*. 

He carried my metaphor of rough gemstones and polish like we were students in *his* classroom. "Absolutely! You know, so many people try to look smart but they really have no idea what they're talking about!"
He became so caught up in rough drafting that even the most direct irony I dared imply went right over him, then straight back out of his mouth.

Absolute defeat.

For a generous spin: [[Conversation as a generative drafting tool.]]

Regardless of how you do it, it's wildly beneficial to get the first draft out. If these people were to ever revisit those concepts, I'm sure they'd be much more tolerable the 2nd go around. 
Articulation rapidly refines thinking. Just pay attention to who's on the receiving end lest you bug the crap out of the wrong audience as a rough drafter.
