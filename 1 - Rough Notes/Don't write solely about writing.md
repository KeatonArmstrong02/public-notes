# Don't write solely about writing
I love meta-learning. It's one of my greatest passions and interests, it's become a large part of who I am. It's also a turpentine effect. (Now that I know Who Dan Koe is, I know who I am) 
[[Venkatesh Rao]] mentions John Eliot in Overachievement the study of "Performance" and "Training" mindset in psychology

> {Training} involves meta-cognition and continuously monitoring your own performance. {Performance} involves an ability to shut off the meta-cognition and just get lost in ‘doing’. Great teachers were probably great learners. Great doers may be slower learners, but are great at shutting off the meta-cognition.

I believe I fall heavily on meta-cognition and optimization. Language learning, Philosophy in journaling, Writing, Traveling. In trying to be meta-aware of the subjects I learn, I can only actually talk about them from a top-down perspective because that's what I understand well. In some cases, I have been *IN* the application of what I'm doing, but still not to the degree of the authority I feel I have access to (This is when citation matters).

So this brings me back to writing. *I think it's dangerous for me to write too closely to my interests when my interests are top-down*. The only thing I want to apply the learning to is more of the skill, not an application of it.

Either you have real authority or you can credit the ideas.


This creates an economy of teaching at the beginning of a skill, marketers like Dan Kow, KNOW that 95% of the market is beginners, so he knows to focus on them, while funneling up to higher ticket for higher skill levels (Iman Gadzhi also does this)
