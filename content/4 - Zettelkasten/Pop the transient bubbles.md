-----

One thing I hate about living in a city is just how transient it all is.

We’re plugged into so many people all the time we’ve developed little deflective bubbles around ourselves. We passively engage without draining any sort of energy and continue going about our business.

This sucks. Yet my time in sales and my time experimenting with extraversion in Austin have led me to the following conclusions:

Pop bubbles. Say something stupid, crash a conversation you’re eavesdropping on, poke fun at your friend's bad idea but then add a dumber one to it. Compliment a stranger. Criticize a stranger. Tell a stranger your weird assumption about them based on a sticker they’ve got. Shatter someone’s expectations then fit into a stereotype so perfectly it’s funny. 

Stop predicting people and start testing them.

We don’t need serendipitous, smooth interactions to meet people in the real world. We need awkward bumbling, we need to feel the tension that they’ll think we’re weird but then be okay with it anyways. We need to be reminded we can see each other. In a transient place, reminders that you even exist is more than most will give you. The bar for standing out has never been so low. Raise someone out of the sea of non-existence. They will like you for it.

Take social risk. At the very worst if you offend someone somehow, they’ll ignore you and pretend you don't exist, and that’s already the default.
