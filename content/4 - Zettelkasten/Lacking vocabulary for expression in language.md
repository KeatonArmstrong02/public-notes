# Lacking vocabulary for expression in language

When children are first learning to speak, they can only use the words they know to formulate sentences. Children usually express pretty simple ideas, but sometimes you can see the frustration when they want to express something they don't quite have the right words for.

Now experience the difficulty of expressing yourself in a foreign language. Frustrated at not expressing how they would normally be able to, most adults give up and revert to their first language to communicate.

Now imagine you didn’t have that first language to fall back on. You wouldn’t know what word to search for, no placeholder for a definition of this idea you want to communicate. Your only choice is to use the words you have at your disposal, risking inaccuracy and confusion.

All thinking is subject to this limiter. If you lack proper vocabulary, you will either have to inaccurately express it, or it stays in abstraction.

Fluid grammar and a robust vocabulary can overcome many of these difficulties, as you could reverse engineer the definitions of words you don’t yet know, could get a little spotty but gets the job done.

This concept revealed itself to me through comparison with an L2. However, now that I recognize it, it also has implications for L1 only. It's hard to recognize when you identify English as your only source of articulation for thoughts, but your skill in English as a medium decides what kinds of thoughts you are even capable of.
